        How a vigil works

        Three steps, then pray.

        1. 1

          Open a shrine

          Name it after what is down, or let the rack name it. It is ready in one click, with no account and nothing to install.

        2. 2

          Send the link

          It unfurls in Slack, Teams or Discord with who is praying and how long you have been down. Everyone who opens it kneels at the same altar.

        3. 3

          Pray, then say they’re back

          Incense, bowl, bows, offerings and prayers, live. When the servers return, press They’re back: the shrine files the vigil and hands you the card to post.

        Rituals

        Things to do while waiting.

        Burn a blame

        Write what happened and burn it in the censer. The words never leave your browser; the room only learns that a confession was burned. Blameless, with a flame.

        Draw a fortune

        An omikuji for engineers: five levels from Great blessing to Great curse, with a line about the deploy, the night on call and plain luck. One per vigil.

        Bow in chorus

        Three or more people bowing within four seconds earn the room a peal of bells and bonus karma. The shrine counts it, so nobody can fake it alone.

        Needs a team

        A shrine that remembers

        Every vigil survived hangs a paper lantern above the altar. Karma grows the shrine itself: a torii gate, a lucky cat on the table, a golden censer.

        Made for the incident channel

        The link does the inviting.

        • Live previews. The pasted link says how long you have been down, who is praying and the last prayer, every time it is unfurled.
        • A badge for the README. One line of Markdown shows the room's state on any status page.
        • A screen for the war room. Add ?tv=1 and the altar fills the wall with the timer and a QR code to kneel.

        Make it your maintenance page

        Give this page a title and a time, put the link on your status page, and refreshing becomes a ritual.
